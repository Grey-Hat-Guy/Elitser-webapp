Metal Elephant Globe

Strength Meets Possibility

It is a traditional Indian Rajasthani handicraft, primarily manufactured by artisan communities in cities like Jaipur and Jodhpur that are famous for their detailed hand-painted iron and metalwork. For centuries, the elephant has held a distinctive place in Indian art and culture, associated with strength, wisdom and prosperity. Paired with the globe, it becomes a symbol of strong foundations and possibilities that extend beyond borders. Rather than tying this piece to an unverified regional craft, we chose it for what it represents to us: the strength of relationships that continue to grow, travel and create new possibilities.

Copper Water Bottle

A Tradition Shaped by Hand

India's copper-working tradition remains particularly renowned in Jandiala Guru, Punjab (officially recognized on the UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage List), where generations of Thathera craftspeople have shaped metal sheets through heating and repeated hammering into functional vessels. According to Ayurveda, drinking water that has been stored undisturbed inside a raw copper vessel overnight for 6 to 8 hours naturally balances the body's three vital energies - the Doshas (Vata, Pitta, and Kapha). Before modern plumbing or filtration systems existed, this daily morning lifestyle anchor acted as a primary source of wellness, digestion stimulation, and structural vitality. Our bottle carries forward that enduring idea: something useful, lasting and made to remain relevant with time.

Coconut Pen Stand

Nature, Given Another Purpose

Originating from Alappuzha (Alleppey), Kerala — the land of coconuts, and Tamil Nadu, this craft was historically a vital practice of zero-waste household living among coastal farmers. The craft traditionally involves cutting, carving, repeated sanding and polishing. What once protected a coconut begins a completely different life — which is why we chose it to represent resourcefulness, transformation and the ability to see new possibilities in the familiar.

Wooden Chair Coaster

A Place at the Table

This specific handicraft originates from Saharanpur, a city in Uttar Pradesh, India. Saharanpur is globally celebrated as India's "Wooden City" due to its centuries-old legacy of intricate woodcarving and artisanal woodworking. The region specializes in transforming dense local hardwoods into functional household utilities and miniature art pieces. The coaster set is meticulously crafted by hand through a traditional multistep process where artisans primarily carve these pieces using durable, seasoned Sheesham (Indian Rosewood) or Mango wood. In Indian culture, greeting guests with water or tea is an essential tenet of hospitality. Incorporating art into something as routine as passing a beverage coaster elevates the act into an expression of respect and welcome. Every enduring relationship begins somewhere often with people sitting together, exchanging ideas, solving problems and learning to trust one another. We chose this handcrafted little chair as a reminder that after 20 years, there is always room at the table for the next conversation.

Talapatra-Inspired Scroll

A Message Across Time

The tradition became especially significant in regions such as Odisha, where palm leaves were also used for intricately illustrated manuscripts. The word itself comes from Sanskrit, where "Tala" means palm and "Patra" means leaf. Long before messages arrived instantly, knowledge in India was recorded and preserved on palm leaves for thousands of years. Before the advent of modern paper, sacred texts and administrative records were meticulously etched onto these leaves. This scroll takes inspiration from this remarkable way of carrying words across generations — only this time, an old form of storytelling leads through a QR code into a digital one. Because the way we communicate changes; the value of staying connected doesn't.
